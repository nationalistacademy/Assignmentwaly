❓ Question 1 (Long Question - 10 marks): Define Iman (Faith) in Islam. Explain the six articles of faith (Iman-e-Mufassal) in detail.

📌 Answer:

Definition of Iman (Faith): Iman is the firm belief and conviction in the heart, affirmed by the tongue, and acted upon by the limbs. It is the foundation of Islam. The Holy Quran says: "The believers are only those who, when Allah is mentioned, their hearts become fearful, and when His verses are recited to them, it increases them in faith; and upon their Lord they rely" (Surah Al-Anfal, 8:2).

The Six Articles of Faith (Iman-e-Mufassal):

  • 1. Belief in Allah (Tauheed): The most fundamental article is belief in the Oneness of Allah. He is the Creator, Sustainer, and Master of the universe. He has no partner, no son, no parents. He is All-Powerful, All-Knowing, and Most Merciful. The Quran states: "Say, He is Allah, the One. Allah, the Eternal Refuge. He neither begets nor is born, nor is there to Him any equivalent" (Surah Al-Ikhlas, 112:1-4).
  • 2. Belief in Angels (Mala'ikah): Angels are created by Allah from light (noor). They are sinless, obedient, and invisible. They perform various duties: Jibraeel (AS) brought revelation, Mika'eel (AS) controls rain and sustenance, Israfeel (AS) will blow the Trumpet on Judgment Day, and Azra'eel (AS) takes souls at death. Two angels (Kiraman Katibeen) record every person's deeds.
  • 3. Belief in Divine Books (Kutubullah): Allah revealed holy books to various prophets as guidance for humanity. These include: the Torah (Tawrat) to Musa (AS), the Psalms (Zabur) to Dawud (AS), the Gospel (Injeel) to Isa (AS), and the final, complete, and unchanged revelation—the Holy Quran—to Prophet Muhammad (ﷺ). Muslims believe in all previous books but follow only the Quran as the final authority.
  • 4. Belief in Prophets (Rusulullah): Allah sent prophets to every nation to guide them to the right path. The first prophet was Adam (AS), and the last is Muhammad (ﷺ). There are 124,000 prophets according to Hadith, but 25 are mentioned in the Quran. Muslims believe in all prophets without discrimination. The Quran says: "We make no distinction between any of His messengers" (Surah Al-Baqarah, 2:285).
  • 5. Belief in the Day of Judgment (Yawm al-Qiyamah): This is belief in the final day when all humans will be resurrected for accountability. On that day, every person will be judged according to their deeds. Those with good deeds will enter Paradise (Jannah), and those with evil deeds will be punished in Hellfire (Jahannam). Belief in the afterlife encourages righteous behavior and accountability.
  • 6. Belief in Divine Decree (Qadr): Belief that everything—good or bad—happens by the will and knowledge of Allah. Allah has preordained everything, including life, death, wealth, and poverty. However, humans have free will to choose between right and wrong, and they will be held accountable for their choices. The Quran states: "Indeed, all things We created with predestination" (Surah Al-Qamar, 54:49).
📖 Quranic Reference: "The Messenger has believed in what was revealed to him from his Lord, and [so have] the believers. All of them have believed in Allah and His angels and His books and His messengers, [saying], 'We make no distinction between any of His messengers.'" (Surah Al-Baqarah, 2:285)
❓ Question 2 (Long Question - 10 marks): Explain the five pillars of Islam (Arkan-e-Islam) in detail. What is the importance of each pillar?

📌 Answer:

Introduction: The five pillars of Islam are the foundation of a Muslim's faith and practice. Prophet Muhammad (ﷺ) said: "Islam is built upon five [pillars]: the testimony that there is no god but Allah and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah; establishing prayer; giving Zakat; fasting Ramadan; and Hajj to the House (Kaaba)" (Bukhari & Muslim).

  • 1. Shahadah (Declaration of Faith): The first pillar is the declaration: "Ash-hadu an la ilaha illa Allah, wa ash-hadu anna Muhammadan rasul-Allah" (I bear witness that there is no god but Allah, and I bear witness that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah). This statement affirms the Oneness of Allah and the prophethood of Muhammad (ﷺ). It is the entry point to Islam. Importance: It distinguishes a Muslim from a non-Muslim and is the basis of all other acts of worship.
  • 2. Salah (Prayer): Muslims perform five daily prayers: Fajr (dawn), Dhuhr (noon), Asr (afternoon), Maghrib (sunset), and Isha (night). Prayer includes recitation of Quran, bowing (ruku), prostration (sajdah), and supplication (dua). Importance: Salah connects a Muslim directly to Allah five times a day, purifies the soul, prevents indecency and evil, and provides spiritual discipline. The Quran says: "Indeed, prayer prohibits immorality and wrongdoing" (Surah Al-Ankabut, 29:45).
  • 3. Zakat (Almsgiving): Zakat is the compulsory charity of 2.5% of a Muslim's annual savings (wealth, gold, silver, cash, business assets) given to the poor and needy. It purifies wealth and helps reduce economic inequality. Importance: Zakat teaches compassion, generosity, and social responsibility. It is a right of the poor upon the rich. The Quran mentions Zakat alongside Salah many times, indicating its importance.
  • 4. Sawm (Fasting in Ramadan): Muslims fast from dawn to sunset during the month of Ramadan. Fasting means abstaining from food, drink, smoking, and sexual relations, as well as evil thoughts and actions. Importance: Fasting develops self-discipline, empathy for the hungry, spiritual purification, and gratitude to Allah. The Quran states: "O you who have believed, fasting has been prescribed upon you as it was prescribed upon those before you, that you may become righteous" (Surah Al-Baqarah, 2:183).
  • 5. Hajj (Pilgrimage to Makkah): Hajj is obligatory once in a lifetime for every Muslim who is physically and financially able. It occurs during the 8th to 12th of Dhul-Hijjah. Pilgrims wear simple white garments (Ihram), perform Tawaf (circling the Kaaba), Sa'ee (walking between Safa and Marwa), stand at Arafat, and stone the devil. Importance: Hajj symbolizes unity, equality, and submission to Allah. It erases past sins and renews one's faith.

❓ Question 3 (Long Question - 8 marks): Write a detailed note on the importance and teachings of the Holy Quran.

📌 Answer:

Definition: The Holy Quran is the literal word of Allah, revealed to Prophet Muhammad (ﷺ) through the angel Jibraeel (AS) over a period of 23 years. It is the final and complete revelation, and it is preserved in its original Arabic text without any change.

Importance of the Holy Quran:

  • Primary Source of Guidance: The Quran is the ultimate guide for all humanity in matters of faith, worship, morality, law, economics, politics, and social relations. It provides solutions to every human problem.
  • Last and Complete Revelation: Unlike previous scriptures that were altered, the Quran remains unchanged. Allah Himself promised its preservation: "Indeed, it is We who sent down the Quran and indeed, We will be its guardian" (Surah Al-Hijr, 15:9).
  • Source of Law (Shariah): The Quran is the primary source of Islamic law. Together with the Sunnah of the Prophet (ﷺ), it forms the basis of Shariah, governing all aspects of a Muslim's life.
  • Spiritual Healing and Mercy: The Quran is a source of healing for spiritual and psychological ailments. It brings peace to the heart. Allah says: "And We send down of the Quran that which is healing and mercy for the believers" (Surah Al-Isra, 17:82).
  • Miracle of Prophet Muhammad (ﷺ): The Quran is the greatest miracle given to the Prophet. Its linguistic excellence, scientific facts, historical accuracy, and prophecies prove its divine origin.

Major Teachings of the Holy Quran:

  • Tauheed (Oneness of Allah): The central message is that Allah alone is worthy of worship. No partners, no intermediaries.
  • Risalah (Prophethood): Belief in all prophets, especially Muhammad (ﷺ) as the final messenger.
  • Akhirah (Hereafter): Belief in the Day of Judgment, heaven, and hell.
  • Justice and Equality: The Quran emphasizes justice for all, regardless of race, color, or social status. "O you who have believed, be persistently standing firm for Allah, witnesses in justice" (Surah An-Nisa, 4:135).
  • Moral Virtues: Honesty, truthfulness, patience, forgiveness, humility, charity, and kindness to parents, orphans, and neighbors.
  • Prohibition of Evils: The Quran forbids shirk (polytheism), lying, cheating, murder, theft, adultery, alcohol, gambling, usury (interest), and oppression.
  • Human Dignity: "And We have certainly honored the children of Adam" (Surah Al-Isra, 17:70).
  • Knowledge and Reflection: The Quran repeatedly encourages learning, observation, and contemplation of the universe.
❓ Question 4 (Long Question - 10 marks): Discuss the concept of "Seerat-un-Nabi" (Life of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ). Explain the key characteristics of the Prophet (ﷺ) as a leader, a teacher, and a role model.

📌 Answer:

Definition of Seerat-un-Nabi: Seerat refers to the life, character, conduct, and teachings of Prophet Muhammad (ﷺ). It includes his biography, his sayings (Hadith), his actions, and his approvals. The Quran commands Muslims to follow the Prophet's example: "Indeed, in the Messenger of Allah you have an excellent example for whoever hopes for Allah and the Last Day and remembers Allah often" (Surah Al-Ahzab, 33:21).

Key Characteristics of Prophet Muhammad (ﷺ):

  • As a Leader:
    • Truthfulness and Honesty: Even before prophethood, he was known as "Al-Amin" (the trustworthy). He never lied or cheated.
    • Consultation (Shura): He consulted his companions in matters of state and war, even though he received divine revelation. This teaches democratic leadership.
    • Justice and Equality: He treated all people equally, regardless of wealth or status. Once he said that if his own daughter Fatima stole, she would be punished.
    • Mercy and Forgiveness: After conquering Makkah, he forgave his worst enemies who had persecuted him for 13 years. This is unmatched in history.
  • As a Teacher:
    • Patience and Gentleness: He taught with kindness, never harshness. He said, "Make things easy, not difficult" (Bukhari).
    • Practical Demonstration: He taught by example. He prayed, fasted, gave charity, and interacted with people in front of his companions.
    • Use of Parables and Questions: He used stories and rhetorical questions to make concepts easy to understand.
    • Respect for Students: He listened attentively and never interrupted anyone. He would sit in a circle with his companions, treating everyone equally.
  • As a Role Model:
    • Worship and Devotion: He prayed at night until his feet swelled. He fasted regularly. He was deeply connected to Allah.
    • Family Life: He was an ideal husband and father. He helped with household chores, mended his own clothes, and treated his wives with kindness and respect.
    • Social Relations: He visited the sick, attended funerals, greeted children, and helped widows and orphans. He never rejected an invitation from the poor.
    • Business Ethics: As a merchant, he was known for fair dealings. He forbade cheating, hoarding, and false oaths.
    • Bravery and Patience: He faced persecution, boycotts, and wars with unwavering patience and trust in Allah. He never sought revenge.
🕌 Quranic Command: "And indeed, you are of a great moral character" (Surah Al-Qalam, 68:4). This verse summarizes the Prophet's (ﷺ) exemplary character.
❓ Question 5 (Long Question - 10 marks): Define Hadith. Explain the classification of Hadith (Sahih, Hasan, Da'if, Mawdu') and the importance of Hadith in Islamic law.

📌 Answer:

Definition of Hadith: Hadith refers to the sayings, actions, approvals, and descriptions of Prophet Muhammad (ﷺ). While the Quran is the word of Allah, Hadith is the practical interpretation and explanation of the Quran by the Prophet (ﷺ).

Classification of Hadith: Based on authenticity, Hadith is classified into four main categories:

  • Sahih (Authentic): A Hadith with a continuous chain of narration (isnad) consisting of upright and precise narrators, free from any irregularity or defect. Example: Most Hadith in Sahih Bukhari and Sahih Muslim. These are used as primary sources of Islamic law.
  • Hasan (Good): A Hadith where the chain of narration is slightly less precise than Sahih, but still acceptable. The narrators may have minor memory issues but are not accused of lying. Example: Many Hadith in Sunan Abu Dawood, Tirmidhi. These can be used for legal rulings but with caution.
  • Da'if (Weak): A Hadith with a break in the chain or a narrator who is known to have a poor memory or is of questionable reliability. Da'if Hadith cannot be used as the basis for legal rulings but may be used for virtuous deeds or moral encouragement, with caution.
  • Mawdu' (Fabricated): A Hadith that is falsely attributed to the Prophet (ﷺ). It contains a known liar in the chain or contradicts established Quranic teachings. Mawdu' Hadith are completely rejected and cannot be used for any purpose.

Importance of Hadith in Islamic Law:

  • Explanation of the Quran: The Hadith explains the general verses of the Quran. For example, the Quran commands prayer but the Hadith explains the method, timings, and number of rak'ahs.
  • Source of Legislation (Second after Quran): The Quran itself commands Muslims to obey the Prophet: "And whatever the Messenger has given you, take; and what he has forbidden you, refrain from" (Surah Al-Hashr, 59:7).
  • Practical Implementation: Without Hadith, many Islamic practices (like how to perform Hajj, Zakat calculation, marriage contracts) would be incomplete.
  • Moral and Ethical Guidance: The Hadith provides detailed guidance on manners, social conduct, and personal development.
🎯 Examiner's Note: For full marks, mention the famous Hadith collections: Sahih Bukhari, Sahih Muslim, Sunan Abu Dawood, Sunan Tirmidhi, Sunan Nasa'i, Sunan Ibn Majah. Also, explain the difference between Hadith and Sunnah briefly.
