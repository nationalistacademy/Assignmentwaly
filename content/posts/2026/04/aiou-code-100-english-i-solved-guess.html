❓ Question 1: Define Parts of Speech. Explain any five parts of speech with examples.

Answer:

🔹 Definition of Parts of Speech:

Parts of speech are the basic categories of words according to their function in a sentence. There are eight parts of speech in English: Noun, Pronoun, Verb, Adjective, Adverb, Preposition, Conjunction, and Interjection.

🔹 Five Parts of Speech with Examples:

1. Noun (اسم):

A noun is a word that names a person, place, thing, or idea.

  • Person: Ali, Sara, teacher, doctor
  • Place: Lahore, school, park, hospital
  • Thing: book, pen, table, car
  • Idea: love, freedom, happiness
  • Examples in sentences: Ali is my friend. I read a book.

2. Pronoun (ضمیر):

A pronoun is a word that is used in place of a noun.

  • Examples: he, she, it, they, we, you, I, me, him, her, them
  • Examples in sentences: He is a good student. She likes tea. Give the book to me.

3. Verb (فعل):

A verb is a word that shows an action or state of being.

  • Action verbs: run, eat, write, play, sleep
  • State of being verbs: is, am, are, was, were
  • Examples in sentences: She runs fast. They are happy. I write a letter.

4. Adjective (صفت):

An adjective is a word that describes a noun or pronoun.

  • Examples: beautiful, tall, red, happy, big, small
  • Examples in sentences: She has a beautiful dress. He is a tall man. The red car is fast.

5. Adverb (قید):

An adverb is a word that describes a verb, adjective, or another adverb. It tells how, when, where, or how often.

  • Examples: slowly, quickly, very, yesterday, there, always
  • Examples in sentences: She runs quickly. He is very intelligent. I met him yesterday.
💡 Memory Tip: Remember the eight parts of speech with this sentence: "Never Play Very Dirty, A Cat Jumps In" (Noun, Pronoun, Verb, Adjective, Adverb, Conjunction, Preposition, Interjection).
❓ Question 2: What are tenses? Explain the Present Indefinite Tense and Past Continuous Tense with rules and examples.

Answer:

🔹 Definition of Tenses:

Tenses are the forms of verbs that show the time of an action or event. There are three main tenses: Present, Past, and Future. Each tense has four aspects: Indefinite (Simple), Continuous, Perfect, and Perfect Continuous.

🔹 Present Indefinite Tense (حال مطلق):

Definition: This tense is used to express habitual actions, universal truths, or general facts.

Rules:

  • Positive Sentence: Subject + Verb (1st form) + s/es (for 3rd person singular) + Object
  • Negative Sentence: Subject + do/does + not + Verb (1st form) + Object
  • Interrogative Sentence: Do/Does + Subject + Verb (1st form) + Object?

Examples:

  • Positive: I eat an apple. He eats an apple.
  • Negative: I do not eat meat. He does not eat meat.
  • Interrogative: Do you like tea? Does she like coffee?
  • Habitual Action: She goes to school every day.
  • Universal Truth: The sun rises in the east.

🔹 Past Continuous Tense (ماضی جاری):

Definition: This tense is used to describe an action that was in progress at a specific time in the past.

Rules:

  • Positive Sentence: Subject + was/were + Verb (1st form + ing) + Object
  • Negative Sentence: Subject + was/were + not + Verb (1st form + ing) + Object
  • Interrogative Sentence: Was/Were + Subject + Verb (1st form + ing) + Object?

Examples:

  • Positive: I was reading a book. They were playing cricket.
  • Negative: I was not sleeping. They were not watching TV.
  • Interrogative: Was she cooking food? Were they coming to the party?
  • Example with time: At 8 o'clock last night, I was watching a movie.

❓ Question 6: Correct the following sentences: (1) He go to school daily. (2) She do not like tea. (3) They was playing football. (4) I have went to Lahore. (5) He is more taller than Ali.

Answer:

Incorrect SentenceCorrect Sentence
He go to school daily.He goes to school daily.
She do not like tea.She does not like tea.
They was playing football.They were playing football.
I have went to Lahore.I have gone to Lahore.
He is more taller than Ali.He is taller than Ali.
💡 Explanations:
  • Subject-Verb Agreement: He, She, It use verb with 's/es' (He goes, She does).
  • Past Tense of 'Be': I/He/She/It was, We/You/They were.
  • Present Perfect Tense: Has/Have + Past Participle (Gone, not went).
  • Comparative Degree: Use 'more' with long adjectives (more beautiful), but not with short adjectives (taller, not more taller).
❓ Question 7: Add correct punctuation marks to the following passage: "he said i am going to lahore tomorrow will you come with me no i said i have some work"

Answer:

Corrected Passage with Punctuation:

"He said, 'I am going to Lahore tomorrow. Will you come with me?' 'No,' I said, 'I have some work.'"

💡 Punctuation Rules Applied:
  • Capital letter at the beginning of a sentence (He, I, Will, No).
  • Comma (,) to separate the speaker from the quote (He said, I said).
  • Period (.) at the end of a statement.
  • Question Mark (?) at the end of a question.
  • Quotation Marks (" " or ' ') around direct speech.
  • Period (.) at the very end of the passage.
❓ Question 8: Write a short story with the moral "Honesty is the Best Policy". You may use the following outline: (A woodcutter's axe falls into the river - a fairy appears - brings gold, silver axes - woodcutter refuses - gets his own axe - rewarded)

Answer:

The Honest Woodcutter

Once upon a time, there lived a poor woodcutter in a small village. He used to cut wood from the forest and sell it in the market. He was very poor but honest.

One day, he was cutting a tree on the bank of a river. Suddenly, his axe slipped from his hands and fell into the deep river. He became very sad because he had no money to buy a new axe. He sat on the bank and started crying.

Suddenly, a fairy appeared from the river. She asked the woodcutter, "Why are you crying, my good man?" The woodcutter told her the whole story. The fairy felt pity for him.

The fairy dived into the river and brought a golden axe. She asked, "Is this your axe?" The woodcutter said, "No, this is not my axe." The fairy dived again and brought a silver axe. She asked, "Is this your axe?" The woodcutter again said, "No, this is not my axe." The fairy dived a third time and brought an iron axe. The woodcutter's face lit up with joy. "Yes, this is my axe!" he said.

The fairy was very pleased with the woodcutter's honesty. She gave him all three axes as a reward. The woodcutter became rich and lived happily ever after.

Moral: Honesty is the Best Policy.

❓ Question 9: Change the voice of the following sentences: (1) She writes a letter. (2) They are playing cricket. (3) The boy broke the window. (4) I will finish the work. (5) Help the poor.

Answer:

Active VoicePassive Voice
She writes a letter.A letter is written by her.
They are playing cricket.Cricket is being played by them.
The boy broke the window.The window was broken by the boy.
I will finish the work.The work will be finished by me.
Help the poor.The poor should be helped.
💡 Rules for Changing Voice:
  • Active: Subject + Verb + Object
  • Passive: Object + helping verb + past participle + by + subject
  • Only transitive verbs (verbs that take an object) can be changed into passive voice.
  • In imperative sentences (commands), use "Let" or "should be".
❓ Question 10: Change the following sentences into Indirect Speech: (1) He said, "I am happy." (2) She said, "Will you come to the party?" (3) The teacher said, "Work hard."

Answer:

Direct SpeechIndirect Speech
He said, "I am happy."He said that he was happy.
She said, "Will you come to the party?"She asked if I would come to the party.
The teacher said, "Work hard."The teacher advised us to work hard.
💡 Rules for Changing Direct to Indirect Speech:
  • Remove quotation marks and use "that" for statements.
  • Change pronouns: I → he/she, you → I/he/she/they (according to context).
  • Change tenses: Present → Past, Past → Past Perfect, etc.
  • Questions: Use "asked" + "if/whether" for yes/no questions; use "asked" + question word (what, where, why) for WH questions.
  • Commands/Requests: Use "ordered/advised/requested" + to + verb.
