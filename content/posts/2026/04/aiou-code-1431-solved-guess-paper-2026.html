❓ Question 1 (Long Question - 10 marks): Define Information and Communication Technology (ICT). Explain the applications of ICT in various fields of life with suitable examples.

📌 Answer:

Definition of ICT: Information and Communication Technology (ICT) refers to all technologies used to handle telecommunications, broadcast media, intelligent building management systems, audiovisual processing and transmission systems, and network-based control and monitoring functions. It is an extended term for Information Technology (IT) that emphasizes the role of unified communications and the integration of telecommunications (telephone lines and wireless signals), computers, necessary enterprise software, middleware, storage, and audiovisual systems.

Applications of ICT in Various Fields:

  • Education: E-learning platforms, online courses, virtual classrooms, digital libraries. Example: AIOU's own Learning Management System (LMS) for online lectures and assignments.
  • Business: E-commerce, online banking, video conferencing, customer relationship management (CRM). Example: Daraz.pk and other online shopping platforms.
  • Healthcare: Telemedicine, electronic health records (EHR), medical imaging, robotic surgeries. Example: Sehat Kahani and other telemedicine apps in Pakistan.
  • Government (E-Governance): Online tax filing (FBR), digital identity systems (NADRA), online passport applications. Example: Pakistan Citizen's Portal for complaint registration.
  • Banking & Finance: ATM machines, online banking apps, mobile wallets (JazzCash, Easypaisa), cryptocurrency.
  • Agriculture: Precision farming, weather forecasting apps, market price information systems, drone-based crop monitoring.
  • Entertainment: Streaming services (Netflix, YouTube), online gaming, social media platforms (Facebook, Instagram, TikTok).
  • Transportation: GPS navigation (Google Maps), ride-hailing apps (Uber, Careem), traffic management systems, airline reservation systems.

❓ Question 2 (Long Question - 10 marks): Define the term "Computer". Describe the five generations of computers in detail, highlighting the key technologies used in each generation.

📌 Answer:

Definition of Computer: A computer is an electronic device that accepts data (input), processes it according to a set of instructions (program), produces information (output), and stores results for future use. It operates under the control of instructions stored in its own memory.

Five Generations of Computers:

  • First Generation (1940-1956): Vacuum Tubes
    - Technology: Used vacuum tubes as the basic components for memory and circuitry.
    - Features: Very large in size (occupied entire rooms), produced excessive heat, consumed huge amounts of electricity, unreliable, expensive.
    - Examples: ENIAC, EDVAC, UNIVAC-1.
    - Languages: Machine language (0s and 1s) and assembly language.
  • Second Generation (1956-1963): Transistors
    - Technology: Replaced vacuum tubes with transistors (invented at Bell Labs).
    - Features: Smaller, faster, more reliable, produced less heat, consumed less power, more portable.
    - Examples: IBM 1401, IBM 1620, UNIVAC 1107.
    - Languages: High-level languages like FORTRAN and COBOL emerged.
  • Third Generation (1964-1971): Integrated Circuits (ICs)
    - Technology: Multiple transistors were placed on a single silicon chip (IC).
    - Features: Even smaller, cheaper, faster, more reliable, lower power consumption.
    - Examples: IBM System/360, PDP-8, PDP-11.
    - Languages: Operating systems became common; keyboards and monitors replaced punch cards.
  • Fourth Generation (1971-1980): Microprocessors
    - Technology: Thousands of ICs were combined on a single chip (microprocessor). Intel 4004 was the first microprocessor.
    - Features: Very small (personal computers), inexpensive, graphical user interfaces (GUIs), networking capabilities.
    - Examples: Apple II, IBM PC, Commodore PET.
    - Languages: C, C++, Pascal, and other high-level languages became popular.
  • Fifth Generation (1980-Present): Artificial Intelligence (AI)
    - Technology: Uses ULSI (Ultra Large Scale Integration) technology, parallel processing, and AI.
    - Features: Natural language processing, expert systems, robotics, voice recognition, portable devices (laptops, smartphones, tablets).
    - Examples: Desktops, laptops, smartphones, tablets, AI assistants (Siri, Google Assistant).
    - Languages: Python, Java, JavaScript, C#, and other modern languages.

❓ Question 3 (Long Question - 8 marks): Differentiate between Hardware and Software. Explain the different types of computer software with examples.

📌 Answer:

Hardware vs. Software:

  • Hardware: The physical, touchable components of a computer system (e.g., CPU, monitor, keyboard, mouse, printer, hard disk).
  • Software: The set of instructions or programs that tell the hardware what to do (e.g., Windows, MS Office, games, browsers).

Types of Computer Software:

1. System Software: Software that controls and manages the basic operations of a computer system. It acts as an interface between hardware and application software.

  • Operating System (OS): Manages all hardware and other software. Examples: Microsoft Windows, Linux, macOS, Android, iOS.
  • Device Drivers: Allow the OS to communicate with hardware devices. Example: Printer driver, graphics card driver.
  • Utility Programs: Perform specific tasks related to computer maintenance. Examples: Antivirus software, disk cleanup tools, file compression tools (WinRAR).
  • Language Translators: Convert high-level programming language code into machine language. Examples: Compiler, interpreter, assembler.

2. Application Software: Software designed to help users perform specific tasks or applications.

  • Word Processors: Examples: MS Word, Google Docs, Notepad.
  • Spreadsheets: Examples: MS Excel, Google Sheets.
  • Presentation Software: Examples: MS PowerPoint, Google Slides.
  • Database Software: Examples: MS Access, Oracle, MySQL.
  • Web Browsers: Examples: Google Chrome, Mozilla Firefox, Safari.
  • Email Clients: Examples: Microsoft Outlook, Mozilla Thunderbird.
  • Graphics & Design Software: Examples: Adobe Photoshop, CorelDRAW, Canva.
  • Games & Entertainment: Examples: PUBG, Free Fire, Candy Crush.
❓ Question 4 (Long Question - 10 marks): What is an Operating System? Explain the major functions of an operating system in detail.

📌 Answer:

Definition of Operating System: An operating system (OS) is a collection of system software that manages computer hardware resources and provides common services for computer programs. It is the most important software that runs on a computer. Without an operating system, a computer is useless. Examples: Microsoft Windows, Linux, macOS, Android, iOS.

Major Functions of an Operating System:

  • Process Management: The OS manages the execution of multiple processes (programs in execution). It allocates CPU time to processes, creates and terminates processes, and handles process scheduling and synchronization. Example: When you open MS Word, a browser, and a media player simultaneously, the OS manages which program gets CPU time and for how long.
  • Memory Management: The OS manages the computer's primary memory (RAM). It keeps track of which parts of memory are being used and by which process, allocates memory to processes when they request it, and deallocates memory when processes are finished. Example: When you open a new application, the OS finds free space in RAM and loads the application there.
  • File Management: The OS manages files on storage devices (hard disk, SSD, USB drives). It organizes files into directories/folders, provides file access permissions, and handles file creation, deletion, renaming, and copying operations. Example: Windows Explorer allows you to browse, create, and manage files and folders.
  • Device Management (I/O Management): The OS manages input and output devices (keyboard, mouse, printer, monitor, etc.). It uses device drivers to communicate with hardware devices and handles I/O requests from processes. Example: When you press a key on the keyboard, the OS receives the signal and passes it to the active application.
  • Security & Protection: The OS protects user data and prevents unauthorized access. It implements user authentication (usernames/passwords), file permissions, and protects processes from interfering with each other. Example: Windows login screen and user account control (UAC) features.
  • User Interface (UI): The OS provides an interface between the user and the computer hardware. This can be a Command-Line Interface (CLI) like MS-DOS or a Graphical User Interface (GUI) like Windows.
  • Error Detection & Handling: The OS constantly monitors the system for errors (hardware failures, software bugs) and takes appropriate action to maintain system stability. Example: "Blue Screen of Death" (BSOD) in Windows or error messages when a program crashes.
  • Resource Allocation: The OS allocates system resources (CPU, memory, I/O devices) among multiple users or processes to ensure efficient and fair usage.
❓ Question 5 (Long Question - 10 marks): What is a Computer Network? Explain different types of network topologies with suitable diagrams and examples.

📌 Answer:

Definition of Computer Network: A computer network is a group of interconnected computing devices (computers, servers, printers, smartphones, etc.) that can communicate and share resources with each other. The primary purposes of a network are resource sharing (files, printers, internet connection), communication (email, chat, video calls), and centralized data management.

Types of Network Topologies:

  • Bus Topology: All devices are connected to a single central cable (called the bus or backbone).
    - Advantages: Inexpensive, easy to install, requires less cable.
    - Disadvantages: If the main cable fails, the entire network fails; performance degrades with more devices.
    - Example: Small office or school lab networks in the past.
  • Star Topology: All devices are connected to a central hub or switch. The hub acts as a central connection point.
    - Advantages: Easy to install and manage; if one cable fails, only that device is affected; high performance.
    - Disadvantages: If the central hub fails, the entire network fails; requires more cable than bus topology.
    - Example: Most home and office networks (Wi-Fi routers act as central hubs).
  • Ring Topology: Each device is connected to two other devices, forming a closed loop or ring. Data travels in one direction (or both directions in a dual ring).
    - Advantages: Data transmission is predictable; handles high traffic well.
    - Disadvantages: If one device or cable fails, the entire network fails (unless a dual ring is used); difficult to troubleshoot.
    - Example: Older IBM token ring networks.
  • Mesh Topology: Every device is connected to every other device in the network.
    - Advantages: Highly fault-tolerant (multiple paths for data); no single point of failure.
    - Disadvantages: Very expensive and complex to install and maintain; requires a lot of cabling.
    - Example: Military networks, large banking networks, or the Internet backbone.
  • Tree (Hierarchical) Topology: A combination of bus and star topologies. It has a root node and multiple levels of star-configured networks.
    - Advantages: Scalable (easily expandable); easy to manage and maintain.
    - Disadvantages: If the root node fails, the entire network fails; requires more cabling.
    - Example: Large corporate networks or university campus networks.
