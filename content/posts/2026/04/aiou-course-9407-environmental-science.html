🌍 1. Introduction to Environmental Science (9407)

Environmental Science is the interdisciplinary study of how natural systems work, how humans interact with the environment, and how we can solve environmental problems. It includes ecology, geology, chemistry, and social sciences.

  • Scope: Natural resource management, pollution control, conservation, environmental policies.
  • Importance: Understanding climate change, protecting biodiversity, sustainable development.

✍️ 3. Solved Important Questions (Most Repeated)

Q1: Define ecosystem. Explain its components and functions.
Answer: An ecosystem is a community of living organisms interacting with their non-living environment.
Components: Abiotic (sunlight, water, soil, temperature) and Biotic (producers, consumers, decomposers).
Functions: Energy flow, nutrient cycling, productivity, decomposition.

Q2: What are the major causes of water pollution? Suggest control measures.
Answer: Causes – industrial waste, sewage, agricultural runoff, oil spills. Control – wastewater treatment, bioremediation, strict laws, public awareness.

Q3: Write a note on biodiversity and its importance.
Answer: Biodiversity is the variety of life at genetic, species, and ecosystem levels. Importance: ecosystem services (clean air, water), food security, medicine, climate regulation, cultural value.

🌿 4. Ecosystem – Detailed Notes

Types of Ecosystems: Terrestrial (forest, grassland, desert) and Aquatic (freshwater, marine).

  • Food Chain: Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Eagle
  • Food Web: Interconnected food chains showing multiple feeding relationships.
  • Ecological Pyramids: Pyramid of numbers, biomass, energy (always upright except some exceptions).

Energy flow: Sun → Producers (photosynthesis) → Primary consumers → Secondary consumers → Decomposers. Only 10% energy transfers to next trophic level (10% law).

💧 5. Pollution – Air, Water, Soil

Air Pollution

  • Causes: Vehicles, industries, burning fossil fuels, deforestation.
  • Major pollutants: CO, SO₂, NOx, particulate matter (PM2.5), ozone.
  • Effects: Respiratory diseases, acid rain, global warming, smog.
  • Control: Clean fuels, catalytic converters, afforestation, emission standards.

Water Pollution

  • Causes: Industrial waste, untreated sewage, agricultural runoff (fertilizers, pesticides).
  • Effects: Eutrophication, waterborne diseases (cholera, typhoid), aquatic life death.
  • Control: Sewage treatment plants, bioremediation, reduce plastic waste.

Soil Pollution

  • Causes: Excessive chemical fertilizers, pesticides, industrial waste, plastic.
  • Effects: Loss of soil fertility, groundwater contamination, health risks.
  • Control: Composting, organic farming, phytoremediation, proper waste disposal.

🐅 6. Biodiversity & Conservation

Types of Biodiversity: Genetic diversity, Species diversity, Ecosystem diversity.

Major threats (anthropogenic): Habitat loss (deforestation, urbanization), overexploitation (hunting, fishing), climate change, pollution, invasive alien species.

Conservation strategies:

  • In-situ conservation: National parks, wildlife sanctuaries, biosphere reserves.
  • Ex-situ conservation: Zoos, botanical gardens, seed banks, gene banks.
  • International efforts: CITES, CBD (Convention on Biological Diversity).

🌡️ 7. Climate Change & Global Warming

Greenhouse effect: Natural process where greenhouse gases (CO₂, methane, water vapor) trap heat in Earth's atmosphere. Without it, Earth would be too cold.

Global warming: Enhanced greenhouse effect due to human activities (burning fossil fuels, deforestation, agriculture).

Effects: Rising sea levels, extreme weather events, melting glaciers, loss of biodiversity, food insecurity.

Solutions: Renewable energy (solar, wind), afforestation, energy efficiency, international agreements (Paris Agreement, Kyoto Protocol).
