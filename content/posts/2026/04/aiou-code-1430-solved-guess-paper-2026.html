❓ Question 1 (Long Question - 10 marks): Define Statistics. Explain the scope and limitations of Statistics in business decision-making.

📌 Answer:

Definition of Statistics: Statistics is the science of collecting, organizing, analyzing, interpreting, and presenting data to make informed decisions. In business, it helps managers understand trends, predict future outcomes, and minimize risks.

Scope of Statistics in Business:

  • Marketing: Analyzing consumer behavior, market research, and sales forecasting.
  • Finance: Risk analysis, portfolio management, and investment decisions.
  • Production: Quality control, inventory management, and process optimization.
  • Human Resources: Employee performance evaluation and workforce planning.
  • Strategic Planning: Using historical data to set realistic goals and budgets.

Limitations of Statistics:

  • Does not study individuals: Statistics deals with aggregates, not single items.
  • Can be misused: Incorrect data or biased sampling leads to wrong conclusions.
  • Requires expert handling: Proper interpretation needs statistical knowledge.
  • Only quantitative data: Qualitative factors (like employee morale) are hard to measure.
  • Results are probabilistic: Statistical findings are never 100% certain; they provide probabilities.
💡 Tutor's Advice: For 10 marks, explain each point with a real-world business example. Draw a neat table comparing scope vs limitations. Examiners love structured answers!
❓ Question 2 (Long Question - 10 marks): Calculate the Mean, Median, and Mode from the following data:
12, 15, 18, 22, 22, 25, 28, 30, 32, 35. Also, identify which measure of central tendency is most appropriate for this data.

📌 Step-by-Step Solution:

Given Data: 12, 15, 18, 22, 22, 25, 28, 30, 32, 35 (n = 10)

1. Mean (Arithmetic Average):
Mean = (Sum of all values) / n
Sum = 12+15+18+22+22+25+28+30+32+35 = 239
Mean = 239 / 10 = 23.9

2. Median (Middle Value):
Since n=10 (even), Median = average of (n/2)th and (n/2 +1)th values.
5th value = 22, 6th value = 25
Median = (22 + 25)/2 = 23.5

3. Mode (Most Frequent Value):
The value 22 appears twice. All others appear once.
Mode = 22

Most Appropriate Measure: The Median is most appropriate here because the data has a slight skew (mean > median), and median is resistant to outliers. However, for business decisions, mean is commonly used for normally distributed data.

📐 Formulas: Mean = Σx / n   |   Median = (n+1)/2th term   |   Mode = most frequent value
❓ Question 3 (Long Question - 8 marks): Explain different types of graphs used in frequency distribution. Highlight their importance in business data analysis.

📌 Answer: Frequency distribution graphs visually represent how often each value occurs in a dataset. They are crucial for business presentations and trend analysis[reference:1].

Types of Graphs:

  • Histogram: A bar chart showing continuous data. Bars touch each other. Use: Employee salary distribution.
  • Bar Chart: Displays categorical data with separated bars. Use: Comparing sales across products.
  • Pie Chart: Circular chart showing proportions. Use: Market share analysis.
  • Frequency Polygon: Line graph connecting midpoints of histogram bars. Use: Tracking monthly sales trends.
  • Ogive (Cumulative Frequency Curve): Shows cumulative frequencies. Use: Finding percentiles, quartiles.

Importance in Business:

  • Quick Insights: Graphs reveal patterns that numbers hide.
  • Decision Making: Managers grasp trends faster through visuals.
  • Reporting: Professional charts enhance presentations.
  • Forecasting: Historical patterns help predict future performance.

❓ Question 4 (Long Question - 10 marks): Differentiate between Sample and Population. Discuss the merits and demerits of sampling in business research.

📌 Answer: Understanding the difference between population and sample is fundamental to business statistics.

Definitions:
Population: The entire group under study (e.g., all customers of a company).
Sample: A subset of the population selected for analysis (e.g., 500 customers).

Merits of Sampling (Why we use samples):

  • Economical: Sampling requires fewer resources, reducing costs[reference:2].
  • Time-Saving: Studying a sample is faster, especially for large populations[reference:3].
  • Feasibility: Practical when the population is too large for complete enumeration[reference:4].
  • Efficiency: Data analysis is easier with smaller datasets[reference:5].
  • Reduced Workload: Focus on fewer data points ensures better quality[reference:6].

Demerits of Sampling (Limitations):

  • Sampling Errors: The process may lead to bias or incorrect inferences[reference:7].
  • Non-Representativeness: If sample isn't representative, results are misleading[reference:8].
  • Limited Accuracy: Results can differ from actual population due to random variation[reference:9].
  • Not Suitable for Small Populations: Complete enumeration is better when population is small[reference:10].
  • Dependence on Sampling Method: Reliability heavily depends on the technique used[reference:11].
📊 Key Insight: A well-chosen sample can accurately represent a population with 95%+ confidence.
❓ Question 5 (Long Question - 10 marks): Define Primary and Secondary Data. Distinguish between them with suitable examples. Also explain their sources in business context.

📌 Answer: Data is the raw material of statistics. It is classified into two main types[reference:12].

Primary Data: Data collected directly by the researcher for a specific purpose[reference:13].

  • Sources: Surveys, questionnaires, interviews, experiments, direct observation[reference:14].
  • Example: A company conducting a customer satisfaction survey to understand its service quality[reference:15].

Secondary Data: Data collected and published by others for purposes other than the current research[reference:16].

  • Sources: Government reports (census), published research papers, business reports, online databases[reference:17].
  • Example: Using a market research report to analyze industry trends.

Differences between Primary and Secondary Data:

  • Originality: Primary is original; Secondary is already collected.
  • Time/Cost: Primary is time-consuming and expensive; Secondary is quick and cheap.
  • Accuracy: Primary is more accurate and relevant; Secondary may be outdated.
  • Control: Researcher has full control over primary data; No control over secondary data.
  • Purpose: Primary is collected for a specific problem; Secondary was collected for another purpose.
