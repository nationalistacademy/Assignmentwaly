# Assignmentwaly multi-agent tasking

This is a human-supervised operating specification, not an active autonomous deployment. Until an agent runtime and its credentials are deliberately configured, tasks are run manually by separate assistants or reviewers. Never expose credentials in prompts or source files.

## Core rule: tool before article
A new article is not ready for publication until its companion free resource exists and has passed a working test. A companion may be a browser tool, calculator, worksheet, checklist, template, downloadable practice set, or guided interactive example. If no meaningful companion can be built, the editor must document why and approve an exception. Never publish a fake or non-functional tool link.

## Agent roles and hand-offs

### 1. Research analyst
Define the learner problem, audience, course/jurisdiction context, search intent and evidence base. Deliver a research brief, primary/official sources, source dates, conflicting claims and unanswered questions. Prefer official AIOU notices, laws, government sources, original research and authoritative documentation. Never invent citations, dates, quotes, course rules, statistics or legal interpretations.

### 2. Free-tool product builder
Convert the learner problem into a small useful free companion before article drafting. Deliver user story, feature scope, working browser-first tool or printable resource, test cases and privacy note. Prefer static/browser-first implementations and no-cost dependencies. Do not require paid APIs or accounts for basic use. Do not send student answers or personal details to a server without approved privacy/security design.

### 3. Content writer
Write an original guide after reading the research brief and testing the companion. Deliver a draft with clear headings, examples, limitations, source links, last-reviewed date and direct link to the working companion. Write from understanding, not by spinning or paraphrasing competitor pages. Attribute borrowed facts and quotations. Do not promise marks, grades, legal outcomes or search rankings. Never misrepresent student authorship.

### 4. Fact-check and integrity editor
Independently verify claims, sources, calculations, examples, attribution, originality and academic/professional boundaries. Deliver claim-to-source checklist, corrections, copyright concerns and approval/rejection reasons. Do not attempt to evade Google policies or AI detectors. No detector score is a quality guarantee.

### 5. SEO, accessibility and UX reviewer
Check whether the page solves the searcher's need and is usable on mobile and assistive technology. Deliver search intent/title/meta review, internal links, structured-data validation where used, mobile checks, keyboard/contrast checks and performance notes. No keyword stuffing, doorway pages, mass-produced thin pages, fake authorship, fake reviews or unsupported EEAT claims.

### 6. Release editor (human approval required)
Review the full package and make the publish/no-publish decision. Confirm the companion is live, free and tested; factual claims are sourced; course dates and rules are current; there is no copied content, fabricated data or fake testimonial; assets are original/licensed/attributed; privacy and integrity boundaries are clear; and the human editor approves publication.

## Standard task packet
Every hand-off includes: working title and intended reader; user problem/search intent; scope/exclusions; required official sources; proposed free companion and acceptance criteria; known uncertainty/review date; output format and hand-off owner.

## Publication states
IDEA → RESEARCHED → TOOL_BUILD → TOOL_TESTED → DRAFTED → FACT_CHECKED → UX_SEO_REVIEWED → HUMAN_APPROVED → PUBLISHED. A failed gate returns the task to the responsible role. No agent may self-approve publication.

## First tool-led backlog
1. Assignment Builder — browser-only draft workspace, Urdu/English direction, question/answer blocks, word count, local draft save, TXT export and print-to-PDF.
2. Study Planner — weekly schedule, task checklist and local progress.
3. Citation Helper — user-entered source details, basic citation formatting and source-verification reminder.
4. MCQ Practice Maker — user-created questions, answer explanations and local practice scoring.

Build one tool at a time, keep it free, independent and accessible, and test it before publishing its companion article.
