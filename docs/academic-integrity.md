# Academic integrity standard

Our education services should help learners understand, practise, and improve their own work.

## Allowed support
- Explain assignment questions and course concepts in accessible language.
- Provide outlines, worked examples, revision notes, quizzes, and practice questions.
- Teach research methods, referencing, citation styles, and responsible use of sources.
- Give feedback on a student's own draft and identify areas that need improvement.
- Help format a student's original work and explain how to cite third-party material.

## Not allowed
- Completing assessed work for a student to submit deceptively as their own.
- Impersonating a student, taking tests or examinations on their behalf, or bypassing proctoring.
- Fabricating references, research data, survey responses, results, or quotations.
- Plagiarism, copyright infringement, selling pirated books, or removing attribution.
- Promising grades, admissions, degrees, or guaranteed examination success.

## Student ownership
Students are responsible for checking institutional rules, verifying facts, citing sources, and submitting work that reflects their own understanding. Where AI tools are used, the learner should follow their institution's disclosure policy.

## Content publication checklist
- Is the explanation original and genuinely useful?
- Are quotations and third-party materials attributed and used lawfully?
- Are official course details and dates verified?
- Does the page explain concepts rather than merely offer a ready-made file?
- Are claims, testimonials, success rates, and credentials evidence-based?
- Is the page clear about whether a resource is a practice example, template, or official document?
